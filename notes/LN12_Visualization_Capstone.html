Lecture 12 Notes — Visualisation & the End-to-End Capstone

Learning outcomes: LO1–LO4 Companion files: slides L12, notebook Week12_Visualization_Capstone.ipynb, the Pipeline Guide

1. Charts are arguments

A truncated bar axis turns a 0.2-star gap between the best and worst city (4% of the 5-star scale) into a dramatic difference. Bars should start at zero. Other tricks to recognise: dual axes that fake correlation, 3-D effects, pies with many slices, cherry-picked windows, and size encodings that exaggerate (doubling a circle's radius quadruples its area).

2. Choosing a chart

Question Good chart Avoid
Compare categories Sorted horizontal bar Pie with many slices
Trend Line 3-D area
Distribution Histogram, box plot Bar of means only
Two numbers Scatter Dual-axis line
Many groups Small multiples One tangled multi-line chart

City means differ by 0.2 stars while the within-city standard deviation is about 1 star, five times larger: show the distribution when it matters.

3. Small multiples and dashboards

One panel per city with shared axes lets the eye compare shapes (quarterly swings of 0.11–0.25 stars). A dashboard has a headline KPI plus a few panels, each answering a stated question; decoration is removed.

4. Quality gates

A quality gate is an automated check that stops the pipeline (non-zero exit) when the data looks wrong. On the Travel-Review pipeline output all seven gates pass:

Gate Observed
Corrupt-line rate below 5% 1.7%
Duplicate rate below 5% 1.1%
Missing or invalid rating below 20% 12.1%
Unparsed dates equal 0 0
Mean rating within 1–5 3.60
Quartiles ordered 3.0 ≤ 3.5 ≤ 4.5
Rating–sentiment correlation positive 0.86

Fed deliberately corrupted statistics, three gates fail as they should. Wire the gate script into run_all.sh so a failure stops publication.

5. The capstone (20% of the grade)

Report structure: (1) problem and users, (2) data and its limitations, (3) pipeline design including cluster configuration, (4) data quality with before/after counts and gates, (5) at least two analytics techniques from Weeks 5–11 with uncertainty, (6) a dashboard following the principles above, (7) ethics and privacy, (8) limitations and next steps, (9) reproducibility.

6. Assessment

End-of-semester examination 70%; Travel-Review Analytics Pipeline 20%; concepts quiz 10%. Weekly labs are ungraded practice for all three.

7. Discussion questions

  1. Find a misleading chart in a news story and redraw it honestly.
  2. Which quality gate would you add for a streaming source, and what threshold?
  3. Which two analytics techniques best suit your capstone question, and why?
