Lecture 10 Notes — Social Network Analytics

Learning outcomes: LO3, LO4 Companion files: slides L10, notebook Week10_Social_Network_Analytics.ipynb, data follows.csv, traveller_profiles.csv

The follower network is synthetic: four interest communities of 150 travellers plus a few popular hubs, so the right answer for community detection is known.

1. Graphs

Nodes are travellers (600); directed edges are "follows" links (3,167); density 0.0088.

2. Degree and the long tail

Mean followers 5.3, median 4, maximum 88. Most people have few followers and a few have many, so the mean misleads. Hubs dominate how information moves.

3. Three centralities

Measure Question
Degree Who has the most direct connections?
Betweenness Who bridges separate groups? (expensive: sample it)
PageRank Who is followed by important people?

Spearman correlation between followers and PageRank is 0.76; 14 of the top 20 by followers are also in the top 20 by PageRank. Choose by the business question: reach, brokerage or endorsement.

4. Communities with Louvain

Louvain groups nodes to maximise modularity. It found 4 communities of 150 with modularity 0.62 and purity 1.00 against the true interests, without ever seeing the interest labels. Real networks are fuzzier: expect partial agreement.

5. PageRank

PR(v) = (1 − d)/N + d · Σ PR(u)/outdeg(u) over followers u of v, with damping d = 0.85 and dangling nodes sharing their rank. A manual NumPy version agrees with networkx to about 4 × 10⁻⁵.

On Spark RDDs each iteration is a join → flatMap → reduceByKey. Cache the link table and materialise the ranks every iteration, otherwise lineage grows and the job slows dramatically. Spark and NumPy results agree to under 10⁻⁴. Each iteration shuffles, which is why dedicated engines (GraphX, GraphFrames, Pregel) exist.

6. Information diffusion

An independent-cascade simulation (adoption probability 0.12, 300 runs per seed): a random traveller reaches about 3 people, the most-followed about 23, the top-PageRank traveller about 21. Reach depends strongly on the seed and on the adoption probability.

7. Limits

Sampling bias (you rarely see the full network), homophily versus influence, privacy (structure can re-identify users), and cost (all-pairs measures need sampling).

8. Discussion questions

  1. A hotel can seed a discount with one traveller. Which centrality would you use and why?
  2. How could you test whether communities reflect influence or similar tastes?
  3. Why does an iterative algorithm like PageRank suit Spark less well than a single groupBy?
